Am I About to Study Again?

4 minutes

605 Words

Nearly twenty years after leaving school, I signed up to study again. I do not have a success story yet — only an exam behind me, results still pending, and the very real surprise of discovering that the idea still matters to me.

Nearly twenty years after leaving school, I signed up to study again. Not because the timing was suddenly perfect, and not because I had finally reached some neat, confident moment of clarity. If anything, it happened in a much less cinematic way than that. I stopped, thought about it properly, and realised I was running out of good reasons not to try.

Why this time felt different

This was not the first time the idea had crossed my mind. I had thought about going back to study more than once over the years, usually in that vague way people think about things they care about but never quite make room for. Work was busy. Life was busy. There was always another priority that felt more immediate.

Those reasons were not fake. They were real, and practical, and easy to defend. But after a while I had to admit that they were also keeping the decision safely theoretical. The longer I left it there, the easier it became to say maybe later.

This time, later did not feel like a plan. It felt like avoidance.

The kind of study I can actually live with

Part of what made it possible was the format. The course is asynchronous, which changed everything for me. No fixed timetable means no rushing from one place to another, no trying to force my life into a shape it does not naturally fit.

That flexibility is a huge advantage, but it comes with its own price. When nobody is waiting for you in a classroom, the structure has to come from you. There is no external rhythm to hide behind. You either show up for it on your own or you do not.

I think that is one of the most honest parts of studying as an adult. The challenge is not only understanding the material. It is protecting the space for it.

The maths part was the real shock

To get in, I had to sit a maths exam. That sentence still feels slightly unreal.

I had not touched maths in over twenty years. Back then, I chose arts, and maths quietly disappeared from my life. It became one of those things I associated with an earlier version of myself, not something I expected to meet again later.

I also did not study as much as I wanted to. I was in the middle of another important project, and the time I had imagined I would dedicate to preparation never fully materialised. That part is frustrating to admit, but it is true.

Still, I sat the exam. I showed up with what I had, not with some ideal version of preparation that existed only in my head.

Now comes the waiting

And now I am waiting for the results.

That in-between space is strange. Nothing has been confirmed yet, so there is no tidy conclusion to offer. I do not know how this story ends. I only know that I did something I had postponed for a long time, and that mattered more than I expected it to.

What surprised me most was not the nerves. It was the excitement. I did not know I still had that kind of energy for this — that specific mix of hope, curiosity, and fear that comes with wanting something enough to risk being disappointed.

Maybe that is the real reason this feels worth writing down now, before I know the outcome. Sometimes the important shift is not the result. It is the moment you stop waiting for perfect conditions and decide to try anyway.
